Low-FODMAP reintroduction: a day-by-day schedule

The low-FODMAP diet isn’t meant to be forever. Reintroduction is where you find out which FODMAPs actually bother you, and how much of them you can have, so you can eat as freely as possible again. It’s also the step most people skip.

Before you start

Start once your symptoms have settled on the low-FODMAP diet, usually after 2 to 6 weeks. If nothing improved, FODMAPs may not be your main problem; talk to your doctor or dietitian before going further. A dietitian trained in the diet is the best guide, and the steps below follow the approach they commonly use.

How each test works

  • One group at a time. Keep the rest of your diet low-FODMAP, so any reaction can only come from the food you’re testing.
  • Three days, rising doses. A small serving on day 1, a medium one on day 2, a large one on day 3. Stop early if you react clearly.
  • Log how you feel for the rest of each day: bloating, pain, gas, stool changes.
  • Three rest days of normal low-FODMAP eating before the next test, so one result doesn’t blur into the next. Rest longer if you reacted.

Which foods test which group

Example doses for days 1, 2 and 3. Test one food from each group.

  • Sorbitol: avocado, ¼, ½, then 1 whole. Or blackberries, 5, 10, then 20.
  • Mannitol: mushrooms or cauliflower, ¼ cup, ½ cup, then 1 cup.
  • Lactose: milk, ½ cup, ¾ cup, then 1 cup. Or yogurt in the same amounts.
  • Fructose: honey, 1 teaspoon, 2 teaspoons, then 1 tablespoon. Or mango, ¼, ½, then 1 whole.
  • Fructans (wheat): wheat bread, 1, 2, then 3 slices. Or pasta, ½, 1, then 1½ cups cooked.
  • Fructans (garlic): ¼, ½, then 1 clove.
  • Fructans (onion): 1 tablespoon, ¼ cup, then ½ cup chopped.
  • GOS: canned lentils or chickpeas, ¼ cup, ½ cup, then 1 cup.

Garlic, onion and wheat are tested separately because many people react to one but not the others. If you have celiac disease, skip the wheat test.

A sample schedule

  • Days 1–3: lactose test (milk).
  • Days 4–6: rest, low-FODMAP as usual.
  • Days 7–9: fructose test (honey).
  • Days 10–12: rest.
  • Days 13–15: sorbitol test (avocado).
  • …and so on through mannitol, wheat, garlic, onion and GOS. All eight take about seven weeks.

The order doesn’t matter much. Many people start with a food they miss most.

Reading your results

  • No symptoms at any dose: you tolerate that group. Bring those foods back.
  • Symptoms only at the biggest dose: small amounts are fine. Keep portions modest.
  • Symptoms at the smallest dose: a likely trigger. Keep it low for now and retest in a few months; tolerance can change.

FODMAPs add up, so a few “small amounts OK” foods in one meal can still cause symptoms. Spread them across the day.
